title: How Gleanvolt decides

You don't need to read this page to use Gleanvolt. It explains why Gleanvolt does what it does, which
helps when something looks surprising.

## Gleanvolt only sets one thing

Your inverter decides where electricity comes from and goes to. Your charger charges the car. Gleanvolt
talks to both over your home network, and changes only:

- **how much current the charger may give the car**, from moment to moment, and when it starts and stops;
- **whether the home battery may discharge**, if you use the [battery discharge hold](../../using/battery-hold/).

Everything else, such as when the home battery charges or what goes to the grid, the inverter decides
as it always has.

## The surplus

Gleanvolt reads, every few seconds, what the panels make, what goes to or comes from the grid, what the
home battery does, and what the car takes. From that it works out what the house uses, and the
**surplus**:

> surplus = what the panels make − what the house uses

The surplus is the power the car could take without buying from the grid or emptying the home battery.
The solar modes give the car the surplus, and no more.

**A passing cloud doesn't stop a charge.** Gleanvolt decides on the average of the last three minutes,
and changes the current only in steps of at least one ampere.

## The car's minimum: why charging pauses

A car can't charge on very little current. By the charging standard, the least is **6 A**, which is
about:

- **1.4 kW** on one phase;
- **4.1 kW** on three phases.

So on three phases, a surplus of 3 kW can't charge the car at all. If Gleanvolt kept the charger on at
6 A, the missing 1.1 kW would come from the grid or the home battery. Instead, the solar modes **pause**
the car, and continue when the surplus is enough again. A car that pauses and restarts a few times on
a cloudy day is Gleanvolt working as intended.

The modes deal with this gap in different ways:

- **Solar** pauses.
- **Forecasted** may let the home battery lend the difference, on a day the forecast says it can pay
  back.
- **Solar + grid** and **Targeted** may buy the difference from the grid, so the sun that is there isn't
  wasted.

A restarted charge runs for at least 10 minutes, and a paused one waits a while before it starts again,
so the car isn't switched on and off every few minutes.

## Pausing and standing down

When a mode has to wait, it does one of two things:

- **A short wait** (under 15 minutes): the charger stays on, at 0 A. The car is paused.
- **A longer wait**, such as overnight or until the low tariff: the charger is switched to **Stop**.
  Gleanvolt calls this **standing the charger down**. When it's time, the charger is switched back on.

The Overview says which, and until when.

## The home battery

The home battery and the car both want the sun. Each mode settles that differently:

| Mode | The home battery |
|---|---|
| Solar | Filled first; the car waits |
| Forecasted | Gets the weak morning and evening sun, and is full by 19:00 |
| Solar + grid | Shares the sun with the car |
| Fast (no battery) | Kept out of the charge |
| Targeted | Keeps its priority; the car gets what the battery doesn't need |

Why is the battery's priority worth anything? In the evening, the battery runs your house from the
sun you stored. Every kWh the car took from it during the day is a kWh you buy at night.

## The forecast

With a [Solcast account](../../setting-up/solar-forecast/), Gleanvolt knows roughly how much sun is
still to come today and tomorrow. Forecasted and Targeted use it to plan. Forecasts are wrong
sometimes, so:

- Forecasted plans on a **cautious** estimate, not the most likely one, so that its promise, "the home
  battery is full by evening", holds on most days.
- It compares the forecast with what the panels really make, and adjusts. If the forecast is far off,
  it stops trusting it for the rest of the day.
- Real sun always counts. A sunny moment the forecast missed still goes to the car.

## The grid meter

Everything above depends on the meter at your grid connection measuring all three phases. If it misses
one, the surplus is wrong, and the solar modes may never start: see
[When the grid meter misses a phase](../unmetered-phase/).
