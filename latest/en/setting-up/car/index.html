Your car and its battery level

On the Car page you tell Gleanvolt two things:

  • The car itself: its battery size, how many phases it charges on, and the currents it accepts. These make charging correct, so fill them in whatever car you have.
  • Where its battery level comes from, optionally. With it, you can ask for a charge in percent, such as “80 % by 7:00”, and the Overview shows the car’s battery. Without it, everything works, and you ask for a charge in kWh instead.

The car

Section titled “The car”

On the Car page (or the wizard’s Car step), fill in:

Field What to enter
Name, Make, Model Whatever you like; they are shown, never acted on
Phases How many phases the car charges on. Many smaller cars charge on 1 even from a 3-phase wallbox
Minimum current (A) The lowest current the car accepts. Usually 6. Some cars need 8
Maximum current (A) The car’s on-board charger limit, often 16 or 32
Usable pack (kWh) The battery’s usable size, which the car’s percentage refers to. It is a little smaller than the size in the brochure: an ID.4 Pro has 77 usable of 82
Charge efficiency Leave it at 0.9 unless you know better

Press Save.

Get the phases right

If the car charges on one phase but Gleanvolt thinks it uses three, every power figure is three times too high. Charging still works, but plans finish late and nothing looks wrong. Check your car’s manual.

The battery level

Section titled “The battery level”

Under What reads this car, choose your manufacturer. The page then shows what it needs.

  • Volkswagen
  • Škoda
  • Audi, SEAT, Cupra, Bentley
  • Other cars

Volkswagen cars are read live from volkswagen.de, about every 20 seconds while charging.

  1. Choose Volkswagen. Enter the e-mail and password you use for the Volkswagen app (your VW ID), and the car’s VIN.

  2. Press Save, then Restart (the page offers it).

  3. Back on the Car page, under Account, press Sign in.

  4. Volkswagen e-mails you a code. Type it in One-time code and press Submit code.

  5. Press Ask the car to check that it answers.

Gleanvolt stays signed in after a restart. If Volkswagen ever signs it out, a band across the top of every page tells you, and you sign in again the same way. It doesn’t retry by itself, because repeated sign-ins can get an account locked.

Škoda cars are read through Škoda’s official API, every 5 to 15 minutes.

  1. Choose Škoda, enter the car’s VIN, and press Save, then Restart.

  2. In the MySkoda app, create an API key for this car.

  3. Back on the Car page, paste the key under Account. Gleanvolt checks it, says until when it is valid, and starts reading the car.

A week before the key expires, Gleanvolt reminds you. Renewing it is pasting a new one.

Note

The Škoda connection is built from Škoda’s published documentation and hasn’t been tried on a car yet.

These are read from the VW Group EU Data Act portal. Its readings are usually 2 to 4 hours behind the car, so they suit planning more than watching a charge.

First, in a web browser:

  1. Open the EU Data Act portal and sign in with your brand’s account (myAudi, SEAT or Cupra ID, and so on). Accept the consent screen.

  2. If your car isn’t in the portal’s list, add it there by its VIN.

  3. Go to Data clusters → Vehicle overview → Get customised data. Choose All data and every 15 minutes. The first data can take from a few hours to a couple of days to arrive.

Then in Gleanvolt:

  1. On the Car page, choose Audi · SEAT · Cupra · Bentley · VW commercial. Choose your brand, and enter the account’s e-mail and password. Enter the VIN only if the account has more than one car.

  2. Press Save, then Restart.

  3. Press Ask the car. When the car’s battery level appears, it works.

If the portal shows a new consent screen or asks for a code, Gleanvolt stops reading and tells you. Sign in to the portal in a browser, deal with it there, and restart Gleanvolt.

Choose Another manufacturer — charge only. Charging works exactly the same; you ask for a charge in kWh rather than in percent.

If Home Assistant can see your car’s battery level, it can pass it on to Gleanvolt. That is described in the repository, with the other advanced integrations.

Your password is kept safe, but not encrypted

Section titled “Your password is kept safe, but not encrypted”

The password and keys you enter go into Gleanvolt’s private data folder, readable only by Gleanvolt. They are never shown again. Anyone with a copy of that folder could sign in to your car’s account, so keep backups of it private: see Updating, backup and uninstalling.
