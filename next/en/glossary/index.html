Glossary

A (ampere), current

Section titled “A (ampere), current”

How much electricity flows. The charger is set in amperes. On three phases, each ampere is about 690 W; on one phase, about 230 W.

Battery discharge hold

Section titled “Battery discharge hold”

Stops the home battery from powering the house and the car, while it can still charge from the sun. See Battery discharge hold.

Battery loan

Section titled “Battery loan”

In Forecasted mode, power the home battery gives the car when the sun is just short of the car’s minimum, on a day the forecast says the battery will be refilled.

Charger, wallbox

Section titled “Charger, wallbox”

The box on the wall the car’s cable plugs into. Gleanvolt sets how much current it gives the car.

Charging mode

Section titled “Charging mode”

How Gleanvolt charges the car: Solar, Forecasted, Solar + grid, Fast (no battery) or Targeted. See Which mode should I choose?

Departure, Ready by

Section titled “Departure, Ready by”

When you need the car. Fast and Targeted can plan a charge to finish by then.

Export, import

Section titled “Export, import”

Selling electricity to the grid, and buying it from the grid.

Forecast, p10, p90

Section titled “Forecast, p10, p90”

How much sun Solcast expects. The expected value is as likely to be beaten as missed; p10 is a cloudier outcome (only 1 day in 10 is worse), p90 a sunnier one.

HDO, low tariff, high tariff

Section titled “HDO, low tariff, high tariff”

A two-price electricity contract: cheaper at certain hours (low tariff), usually at night. In the Czech Republic it’s switched by the distributor’s HDO signal. See Grid tariff.

Home battery

Section titled “Home battery”

The battery connected to your inverter, which stores sun for the evening. Not the car’s battery.

House

Section titled “House”

What your house uses, not counting the car. Gleanvolt works it out from the other figures.

Inverter

Section titled “Inverter”

The device that turns your panels’ power into household electricity and manages the home battery.

kW, kWh

Section titled “kW, kWh”

kW (kilowatt) is power: how fast, at this moment. kWh (kilowatt-hour) is energy: how much in total. A car charging at 11 kW for two hours takes 22 kWh.

Minimum current, 6 A

Section titled “Minimum current, 6 A”

The least current a car can charge on: about 4.1 kW on three phases, 1.4 kW on one. Below it, the car pauses. See How Gleanvolt decides.

Modbus TCP

Section titled “Modbus TCP”

The way Gleanvolt talks to the inverter and the charger over your home network. It must be turned on in each device. See Preparing your inverter and charger.

Phase, L1, L2, L3

Section titled “Phase, L1, L2, L3”

Most homes in Europe have three-phase electricity: three separate wires, called L1, L2 and L3 (SolaX: R, S and T). A car charges on one phase or on all three.

Recovery key

Section titled “Recovery key”

A code shown once when you set your Gleanvolt password, to reset the password if you forget it.

Session

Section titled “Session”

One charge, from start to stop. See Sessions and history.

SOC (state of charge)

Section titled “SOC (state of charge)”

How full a battery is, in percent. Used for both the home battery and the car’s battery.

SOC floor

Section titled “SOC floor”

The home battery level Forecasted mode won’t let the car take it below, so it can still be full by the evening.

Solcast

Section titled “Solcast”

The service that provides the solar forecast. Free for home use. See Solar forecast.

Standing down

Section titled “Standing down”

Switching the charger to Stop during a long wait, such as overnight, instead of pausing it.

Surplus

Section titled “Surplus”

The sun your house doesn’t use at this moment: what the panels make minus what the house uses. What the solar modes give the car.

Unmetered grid phase

Section titled “Unmetered grid phase”

A phase your grid meter doesn’t measure. Leave the setting empty unless that is really the case. See When the grid meter misses a phase.

VIN

Section titled “VIN”

The car’s vehicle identification number, 17 letters and digits, shown in the car’s papers and app.
